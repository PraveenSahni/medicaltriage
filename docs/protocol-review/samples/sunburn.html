Sunburn

IST Open-Source Guideline Content | Mixed · Version Year 2026 · Protocol ID AlgorithmID 1010 (oscg-sunburn)

Definition

Damage to the skin caused by too much sun exposure. Usually resolves within about a week and can be managed independently at home, but severe cases can progress to heat exhaustion or heatstroke.

Severity / Key Points

  • Affected skin may feel hot, sore, or painful, and typically flakes or peels a few days after the sunburn occurs.
  • Fair skin usually reddens or turns pink; darker skin tones may show minimal colour change.
  • Severe sunburn can blister.
  • Getting sunburnt increases the risk of skin cancer.

Triage Assessment Questions and Dispositions

#QuestionDisposition
1 Is there confusion, fainting, a very high temperature with no sweating, or a rapid heartbeat suggesting heatstroke rather than simple sunburn? Call Emergency Services Now (LevelID 100)
2 Is the skin blistered or swollen, is the temperature very high or does the caller feel hot, cold, or shivery, does the caller feel very tired, dizzy, or sick, is there a headache or muscle cramps, or is the patient a baby or young child? See Urgent Care Today (LevelID 70)
3 Is this mild-to-moderate sunburn without blistering or the features above? Home Care (LevelID 15)

Care Advice

Emergency heat-illness precautions — Move to a cool place, cool the skin, and arrange emergency transport immediately. Call Back If: worsening confusion, loss of consciousness.

Urgent sunburn review — Cool the skin, stay hydrated, and arrange same-day medical review, especially for blistering or a young child. Call Back If: fever worsens, increasing dizziness.

Home care for sunburn — Get out of the sun, cool the skin with a cool shower or damp towels, apply aftersun or unperfumed moisturiser, stay hydrated, and take paracetamol or ibuprofen as needed. Avoid alcohol and further sun exposure. Call Back If: blistering develops, or fever, dizziness, or feeling very unwell develops.

First Aid

  • Get out of the sun immediately.
  • Cool the skin using a cool shower, bath, or damp towels.
  • Apply aftersun cream or unperfumed moisturiser.
  • Stay hydrated; avoid alcohol.
  • Take paracetamol or ibuprofen as directed if needed.
  • Avoid petroleum jelly, ice packs, scratching, removing peeling skin, or popping blisters.

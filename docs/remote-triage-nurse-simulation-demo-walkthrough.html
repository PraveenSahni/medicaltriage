Remote Triage Nurse simulation walkthrough

A presenter-ready, screen-by-screen demonstration of how a remote triage nurse accepts one call, applies deterministic emergency safety rules, follows acuity-ordered clinical questions, confirms the Qatar disposition and fit-to-fly result, and produces an auditable SBAR handoff.

AudienceBusiness and clinical operations
Primary roleRemote Triage Nurse
EnvironmentSimulation, synthetic data only
Recommended time15 to 18 minutes
Demo boundary: The walkthrough demonstrates the implemented rules-first clinical workflow. It must not be described as autonomous diagnosis, production STCC content, live call-center connectivity, or a substitute for nurse approval.
Before the audience joins

Pre-demo checklist

  • 01Open triages.irisstar.tech and confirm the gold SIMULATION banner is visible.
  • 02Select B - Remote Triage Nurse. The simulation profile populates the assigned role and demo credentials.
  • 03Sign in and verify the Step cockpit loads with queue records. Keep the browser at 100% zoom.
  • 04Use the emergency chest-tightness call for a predictable safety-floor demonstration.
  • 05Do not present synthetic protocols as licensed STCC content. Use “STCC-compatible workflow structure” throughout.

Eight-screen narrative

Live demonstration script

Remote Triage Nurse journey

Each screen includes the exact presenter action, suggested speaking points, proof to highlight, and a note to prevent overstatement.

SCREEN 01 / SIMULATION ENTRY

Establish a safe demonstration boundary

Begin by making it explicit that every visible patient and queue record is synthetic and that the role is a named simulation user.

Presenter action

Point to the SIMULATION banner, organization, language, and named-user selector. Do not sign in as Platform Administrator for this journey.

“We are using a governed simulation environment with synthetic records only. The user’s assigned role determines the screens, actions, and data they can access.”

What to validate

  • Simulation banner is visible.
  • No PHI is used.
  • Role is attached to a named user rather than chosen after login.

Presenter note

Do not describe the simulation selector as the production authentication model. Production will use managed identity and SSO policy.

SCREEN 02 / NURSE ACCESS

Select the Remote Triage Nurse profile

Show the Business and Clinical Operations grouping and select the Remote Triage Nurse named user.

Presenter action

Select B - Remote Triage Nurse, confirm the assigned role changes, then select Simulate user or sign in using the populated simulation profile.

“The nurse does not manually assemble permissions. The profile carries the approved role, and the application exposes only the relevant clinical workspace.”

What to validate

  • Assigned role is remote_triage_nurse.
  • Email changes with the selected simulation user.
  • The nurse lands in the Step cockpit.

Presenter note

Simulation credentials are demonstration data. Never reuse them for a production deployment.

SCREEN 03 / ONE ACTIVE CALL

Accept one caller from the clinical queue

The nurse sees waiting calls, clinical priority, patient type, channel, age, and red-floor indicators before opening one encounter.

Presenter action

Explain the queue filters, select the chest-tightness emergency record, and choose Open triage.

“The nurse receives a prioritized queue, not an unstructured inbox. Opening the case creates one nurse-owned workflow and blocks a second active encounter until this call is completed or placed on hold.”

Operational value

  • Clinical priority is visible before pickup.
  • HRMS-derived age and patient type are already available.
  • One-call ownership supports traceability and queue locking.

Presenter note

Live telephony answer and callback commands are part of the provider-neutral integration design; the current demonstration uses simulated queue events.

SCREEN 04 / REASON AND EMERGENCY RULE-OUT

Confirm the reason for call and apply the safety floor

The first clinical action combines caller context, protocol search preparation, vital signs, and deterministic emergency checks.

Presenter action

Point out the validated age, incoming channel, reason narrative, prepared search words, vitals, and the red safety panel.

“Before normal protocol progression, the rules engine checks non-negotiable red floors. Here, low SpO2 and the heart-rate threshold trigger RED_ALERT. No AI suggestion can downgrade that result.”

What to validate

  • Identity and age are shown as HRMS validated.
  • Search words support protocol matching.
  • The API score is 10 with RED_ALERT.
  • RAG shadow is advisory and does not own the safety decision.

Presenter note

The current protocol content is synthetic/open-source demo content structured for STCC compatibility. Licensed STCC content remains an import and validation dependency.

SCREEN 05 / ACUITY-ORDERED ASSESSMENT

Ask one high-acuity question at a time

The screen reduces cognitive load by showing the single active question and keeping lower-priority items locked.

Presenter action

Read the active question, show that lower-priority questions remain locked, and point to the Yes/No decision effect.

“The nurse is guided through the most serious possibility first. A Yes fixes the relevant disposition; a No unlocks the next acuity item. The nurse remains the accountable decision-maker.”

What to validate

  • Only one question is active.
  • Priority and proposed route are visible.
  • The response defaults to No in the simulation.
  • The emergency floor remains active independently of question progression.

Presenter note

Do not claim that a default No is a clinical answer. The nurse must validate every response captured during the call.

SCREEN 06 / QUESTION PROGRESSION

Preserve an auditable assessment trace

Demonstrate that the sequence progresses only after an answer is recorded and that prior answers remain visible.

Presenter action

Select No on the first question, then show the second question and the recorded assessment trace.

“The workflow records what was asked, what the nurse confirmed, and how the next question was selected. That trace supports clinical review, quality assurance, and later audit.”

Operational value

  • No skipped acuity levels.
  • Visible answer history.
  • Deterministic protocol progression.
  • Human validation stays explicit.

Presenter note

For an emergency-floor case, the route remains Emergency even if subsequent protocol questions are negative.

SCREEN 07 / DISPOSITION AND AVIATION GATE

Confirm the Qatar route and fit-to-fly restriction

The system brings together severity, local facility routing, aviation status, rationale, first aid, and care advice for nurse approval.

Presenter action

Point to Emergency severity, HMC Emergency Department, fit-to-fly Restricted, the governing rule, and mapped care advice.

“The output is more than an acuity label. It combines the approved Qatar disposition route with an aviation gate. Emergency or urgent findings prevent a fit-to-fly clearance until the appropriate clinician completes review.”

Qatar-specific value

  • Local routing to HMC, Sidra, PHCC, teleconsult, or self-care pathways.
  • Pediatric emergency routing can direct to Sidra.
  • Fit-to-fly is synchronized with clinical disposition.
  • Care advice remains linked to the selected disposition.

Presenter note

Local facility mapping and aviation policy must be customer-approved before production. The nurse cannot use fit-to-fly as an independent override of emergency acuity.

SCREEN 08 / SBAR AND COMPLETION

Review the structured handoff and complete the encounter

The final screen consolidates the nurse-validated encounter into an English and Arabic SOAP/SBAR note suitable for governed handoff and clipboard use.

Presenter action

Review Situation, Background, Assessment, and Recommendation. Highlight the rule evidence and final route before completing the case.

“The system converts the validated workflow into a consistent handoff note. The nurse reviews the evidence, approves the disposition, and leaves a traceable record for referral, follow-up, and downstream integration.”

What to validate

  • Caller identity remains masked.
  • Vitals and RED_ALERT rationale are present.
  • HMC route and fit-to-fly restriction match the disposition screen.
  • The nurse, not the AI, owns completion.

Presenter note

Clipboard note generation is implemented. Live EMR/FHIR writeback and national exchange submission require customer endpoint, consent, terminology, and conformance validation.
